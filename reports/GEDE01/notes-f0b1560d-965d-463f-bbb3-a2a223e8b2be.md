## New hypothesis and measured progression

The new hypothesis was that the phase in which an address becomes a pointer
could prevent GC/1.3 from reassociating its field displacement with its index.
Small auto-inlined C access/address helpers alone did not change the baseline.
Union pointer/integer conversions recovered indexed accesses but added stack
stores; the best such diagnostic was 83.30556% at 160 bytes. Those probes are
not the retained reconstruction.

A scalar round trip from pointer to unsigned word, through a 64-bit integer,
and back to pointer recovered the indexed accesses without extra memory
operations. Both signed and unsigned 64-bit intermediates measured 92.5% at
144 bytes. Moving the first conversion to its accesses restored the desired
register assignments but scheduled the first field address too early
(94.44444%). An explicit byte offset corrected scheduling but swapped r29
and r30 (98.611115%). Assigning the first-field pointer at the first load
resolved that allocation difference (100%).

The final macro preserves that expression while eliminating an unused
four-byte out-of-line helper emitted by the initial C helper spelling. The
final object contains exactly one function, fn_801EDE34, starting at offset
zero and occupying 144 bytes. All 36 instructions match. r31 holds the
unindexed global base, r30 the scaled index, and r29 the unindexed first-field
base. This corrects the earlier failed report's description of the baseline:
its r29 held an indexed element address after add/lwzu.

The cast round trip preserves the target ABI's 32-bit pointer representation.
The index parameter passed to fn_8022A118 remains unchanged. The first-field
assignment is evaluated before any short-circuit exit, all three comparisons
retain their order, and the three stores still occur only after the call.
